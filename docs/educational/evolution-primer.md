# Evolution Primer

This guide explains how natural selection and evolution work in PreyLife.

## What is Evolution?

Evolution is change in inherited traits over generations. In PreyLife, creatures inherit genetic attributes from their parents, and natural selection favors traits that improve survival and reproduction.

## The Ingredients of Evolution

Evolution requires four conditions, all present in PreyLife:

### 1. Variation

Individuals differ in their traits.

**In PreyLife:**
- Creatures have different strength, stealth, learnability, and longevity values
- Initial populations start with diverse trait distributions
- Visible as different colors (blue-green for prey, red-yellow for predators)

### 2. Inheritance

Offspring inherit traits from parents.

**In PreyLife:**
- Children start with parents' traits
- Small random variation adds during reproduction
- Traits are passed to the next generation

### 3. Selection

Some traits improve survival and reproduction.

**In PreyLife:**
- High stealth prey avoid detection
- Strong predators catch more prey
- Long-lived creatures reproduce more

### 4. Differential Reproduction

Better-adapted individuals have more offspring.

**In PreyLife:**
- Creatures need energy to reproduce
- Better foragers/hunters have more energy
- More energy = more reproduction opportunities

## How Selection Works

### Survival of the Fittest

"Fitness" means reproductive success, not physical strength.

**Example in PreyLife:**
- A stealthy prey might be weak but avoids predators
- It survives longer and reproduces more
- Its stealth trait spreads through the population
- Stealth-focused prey become more common

### Selection Pressure

Environmental factors create selection pressure:

| Pressure | Favored Traits |
|----------|---------------|
| High predation | Prey stealth/speed |
| Scarce resources | Foraging efficiency |
| Long dry spells | Longevity |
| Many predators | Prey evasion |
| Few prey | Predator hunting ability |

### Adaptive Trade-offs

No trait is universally "best":
- High strength uses more energy
- High stealth may mean lower strength
- Specialization helps in some conditions, hurts in others

## Mechanisms of Inheritance

### Genetic Attributes

PreyLife creatures have four genetic traits:

1. **Strength** (0.0 - 1.0)
   - Affects movement speed
   - Affects hunting/escape success
   - Increases energy consumption

2. **Stealth** (0.0 - 1.0)
   - Affects detection avoidance (prey)
   - Affects prey detection (predators)
   - Affects escape/capture success

3. **Learnability** (0.0 - 1.0)
   - Affects trait adoption rate
   - Allows adaptation without reproduction

4. **Longevity** (0.0 - 1.0)
   - Affects maximum lifespan
   - Affects metabolic efficiency

### Mutation

Random changes occur during reproduction:

**Normal variation:**
- Every offspring has slight random changes
- Range: ±0.05 in each trait
- Creates gradual evolution

**Significant mutation:**
- 10% chance of larger change
- Range: ±0.2 in one trait
- Creates jumps in evolution

### Genetic Drift

In small populations, random chance matters:
- A strong creature might die by bad luck
- A weak creature might survive and reproduce
- Over time, random changes accumulate

## Types of Selection

### Directional Selection

One extreme trait value is favored.

**Example:**
- Environment with many predators
- Higher stealth = better survival
- Over time, average stealth increases
- Population "moves" toward one direction

### Stabilizing Selection

Middle values are favored.

**Example:**
- Moderate strength works well
- Too strong = uses too much energy
- Too weak = can't escape
- Population clusters around optimal middle

### Disruptive Selection

Extreme values are both favored.

**Example:**
- Either very stealthy OR very strong works
- Middle values are worst of both worlds
- Population splits into two groups

## Observing Evolution

### Short-term Changes (10-50 days)

- Individual creatures die and are born
- Slight shifts in average traits
- Color gradients begin changing

### Medium-term Changes (50-200 days)

- Clear trait shifts visible
- Specialized phenotypes emerge
- Population oscillations occur

### Long-term Changes (200+ days)

- Stable strategies dominate
- Multiple specializations coexist
- Equilibrium or extinction occurs

## Evolutionary Strategies

### K-selection vs r-selection

**r-selection (many offspring, less investment):**
- High reproduction rate
- Lower survival rate
- Works when resources abundant

**K-selection (few offspring, more investment):**
- Lower reproduction rate
- Higher survival rate
- Works when competition is high

### Specialization vs Generalization

**Specialists:**
- Excel in one area
- Vulnerable to environmental change
- Visible as extreme colors

**Generalists:**
- Moderate in all areas
- Adaptable to change
- Visible as medium colors

## Unique Mechanisms

### Social Learning

PreyLife includes "horizontal transfer" - learning from others:
- Creatures can adopt traits from neighbors
- Spreads successful strategies quickly
- Can homogenize populations

### Species Conversion

Extreme evolution can cause species changes:
- Prey with extreme traits may become predators
- Predators with weak traits may become prey
- Simulates major evolutionary transitions

## Evolution Activities

### Observation Exercise

1. Run simulation for 200 days
2. Note starting average traits
3. Record traits every 50 days
4. Graph the changes
5. Explain why traits changed

### Selection Experiment

1. Create high-predation environment (many predators)
2. Predict which prey traits will increase
3. Run simulation
4. Compare results to prediction

### Bottleneck Simulation

1. Run until stable population
2. Pause and remove 90% of prey
3. Resume and observe
4. How do surviving traits affect future population?

### Adaptation Race

1. Set up challenging environment
2. Predict which species adapts faster
3. Explain based on selection pressure
4. Test your hypothesis

## Key Concepts

### Fitness Landscape

Imagine a landscape where height = fitness:
- Peaks = successful trait combinations
- Valleys = unsuccessful combinations
- Evolution climbs toward peaks
- Multiple peaks = multiple strategies work

### Red Queen Hypothesis

Species must constantly evolve to survive:
- Prey evolve better escape → predators must evolve better hunting
- Neither can stop evolving
- Constant "arms race"

### Punctuated Equilibrium

Evolution may not be gradual:
- Long periods of stability
- Sudden rapid change
- Environmental shifts trigger jumps

This is visible in PreyLife when:
- Stable populations suddenly crash
- Species conversion events occur
- Resource blooms change selection pressures
